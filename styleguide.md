# CV Portfolio Copy Styleguide

## Purpose

This site presents Jeremy Osztreicher’s software engineering work and creative technology practice.

It should work for:
- senior frontend / platform engineering roles
- cultural, media, archive, and arts technology contexts
- people looking for technically credible creative collaborators

The site should not read like a job-search landing page or an artist statement padded with abstractions.

## Durable Identity

Use the same base identity across the site:

> Jeremy Osztreicher  
> Software engineer. Creative systems builder.

This should remain true whether Jeremy is interviewing, consulting, collaborating, or documenting public work.

Avoid making the identity depend on audience-specific framing.

Do not use:
- currently seeking
- available now
- hire me
- open to opportunities
- passionate about
- where engineering meets creativity
- at the intersection of

## Core Positioning

Jeremy builds and maintains production software, with a parallel practice in creative tools, cultural interfaces, sound, archives, and artist-facing web work.

The point is not that these are two unrelated halves. The same qualities should come through in both:

- clear interfaces
- reliable delivery
- useful abstractions
- attention to how people actually use software
- taste without vagueness
- systems that can be maintained, not just demoed

## Content Spine

Every page variation should keep the same basic structure:

1. Name
2. Short tagline
3. One supporting paragraph
4. Brief shared positioning
5. Engineering proof
6. Creative systems proof
7. Project inventory
8. Contact / links

Route variations may change emphasis, pacing, visual treatment, and project ordering.

They should not invent a different Jeremy for each audience.

## Hero Copy

Hero taglines should be short and concrete.

Good:

> Software for cultural systems.

> Frontend engineering and creative tools.

> Production software. Public experiments.

> Interfaces, archives, instruments.

> Shipped systems and strange tools.

Avoid:

> Practical systems with a strong point of view.

> A multidisciplinary practice spanning engineering and creativity.

> Where technical rigour meets creative exploration.

> A portfolio for hiring teams and artistic collaborators.

A heading should not explain the whole site. If it needs more than seven words, move it into body copy.

## Supporting Paragraphs

Supporting paragraphs should be one or two sentences.

They should answer:

- What kind of work is this?
- Where has it been used?
- What can the reader trust Jeremy to do?

Good:

> I work across production frontend engineering, platform architecture, cultural data products, archive interfaces, and browser-based creative tools.

> My work ranges from React and TypeScript systems in large organisations to public experiments with sound, astrology, calendars, and archives.

> I build software that has to be used, maintained, and understood — whether it is part of a product platform or a public creative tool.

Avoid:

> My practice explores the relationship between technical systems, cultural production, and creative possibility.

> The work is united by a commitment to thoughtful digital experiences.

> This portfolio frames a body of work across engineering and creative practice.

## Copy Rules

Use plain, durable language.

Prefer:
- built
- shipped
- maintained
- led
- designed
- improved
- integrated
- documented
- prototyped
- production
- public
- internal
- browser-based
- artist-facing
- archive
- sound
- interface
- platform

Avoid:
- throughline
- intersection
- explores
- reimagines
- unlocks
- empowers
- seamless
- dynamic
- innovative
- impactful
- robust, unless technically justified
- scalable, unless technically justified
- thoughtful, unless followed by specifics
- strong point of view
- body of work, unless the page genuinely reads like an artist portfolio

## Human Sentence Test

Every sentence should do at least one real job.

It should say one of the following:

- what was built
- what was shipped
- what was maintained
- what problem was solved
- what system it touched
- who used it
- what technical judgement was involved
- what kind of collaboration it supports

If a sentence only summarises the vibe, cut it.

Weak:

> A set of projects united by practical systems thinking.

Better:

> Projects include production frontend work, cultural calendars, browser sound tools, archive interfaces, and astrology-based visual systems.

Weak:

> Creative systems with an editorial point of view.

Better:

> Public experiments using Web Audio, cultural datasets, symbolic systems, and browser-native interaction.

Weak:

> Work that bridges engineering and creative practice.

Better:

> Production React and TypeScript work sits alongside browser instruments, archive explorers, and artist-facing tools.

## Anti-AI Copy Rules

Cut or rewrite anything that sounds like a summary of itself.

Avoid:

- “the throughline is…”
- “this section highlights…”
- “this route positions…”
- “the work sits at the intersection…”
- “a body of work spanning…”
- “designed to showcase…”
- “creative yet practical…”
- “technical and artistic sensibility…”
- “a strong point of view…”
- “thoughtful digital experiences…”

Instead, name the work.

Bad:

> The throughline is practical systems with a strong point of view.

Better:

> The work includes production frontend systems, cultural data tools, browser instruments, archive interfaces, and artist-facing web projects.

Better, shorter:

> Production software, cultural tools, sound systems, archive interfaces, and artist-facing web work.

Better, more personal:

> I build production software and public-facing creative tools: frontend systems, cultural calendars, sound sketches, archive interfaces, and esoteric web experiments.

## Shared Positioning Line

Use one of these depending on tone.

Plain:

> Production software, cultural tools, sound systems, archive interfaces, and artist-facing web work.

Slightly more personal:

> I build production software and creative tools for cultural, technical, and artist-facing contexts.

More engineering-weighted:

> Senior frontend and platform engineering, with public work in cultural data, sound, archives, and creative interfaces.

More creative-weighted:

> Software engineering for cultural systems, browser instruments, archive explorers, and artist-facing tools.

Avoid adding a second sentence that explains the “meaning” of the list. Let the nouns carry the weight.

## Engineering Panel

Purpose: show technical credibility without turning into a keyword dump.

Good:

> Senior product engineering across React, TypeScript, APIs, design systems, platform architecture, AWS/serverless, and delivery inside large organisations.

Alternative:

> Production frontend and platform work: React, TypeScript, design systems, API boundaries, serverless services, technical direction, and pragmatic delivery.

Avoid:

> Engineering evidence

> Robust, scalable, high-quality technical solutions

> Technical excellence across the full product lifecycle

Panel labels:

Good:
- Production engineering
- Technical delivery
- Frontend and platform work
- Systems that ship

Avoid:
- Engineering evidence
- Studio proof
- Capability layer

## Creative Systems Panel

Purpose: make the creative work sound serious without making it pompous.

Good:

> Public tools and experiments using Web Audio, browser instruments, cultural datasets, astrology, archive structures, live-coding references, and symbolic interfaces.

Alternative:

> Browser-based creative work: sound sketches, cultural calendars, archive explorers, astrology interfaces, and small public tools.

Avoid:

> Software as a studio medium

This is not bad as an idea, but it sounds like artist-statement filler unless the surrounding copy is very concrete.

Panel labels:

Good:
- Creative technology
- Browser instruments and public tools
- Cultural and sound projects
- Artist-facing systems

Avoid:
- Studio evidence
- Creative systems section
- Practice output

## Contact Block

The contact block should be direct but not salesy.

Good:

> Strong fit for senior frontend/platform engineering, cultural and media products, design-system adjacent teams, creative tooling, archive interfaces, and artist-facing software.

Better, less LinkedIn:

> Relevant for frontend/platform engineering, cultural and media products, creative tools, archive interfaces, and teams that care about both delivery and taste.

More personal:

> Useful for teams building cultural products, frontend platforms, creative tools, archive systems, or artist-facing software.

Avoid:

> Let’s build something amazing together.

> Available for exciting opportunities.

> Open to visionary collaborations.

## Project Captions

Captions should start with scope, status, or context.

Good:

> Public calendar for London dance classes and events.

> Browser-based astrology chart renderer using D3 and whole-sign houses.

> Sound sketch using live input, modulation, and browser audio.

> Internal frontend/platform work across React, TypeScript, APIs, and design-system delivery.

Avoid:

> A poetic exploration of time, motion, and embodied practice.

> A creative investigation into symbolic systems.

> An innovative tool for cultural discovery.

A project can be poetic. The caption should still tell people what it is.

## Rewrite Defaults

Use these replacements as defaults:

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| The throughline is practical systems with a strong point of view. | Production software, cultural tools, sound systems, archive interfaces, and artist-facing web work. |
| Engineering practice, studio output. | Software engineering work and creative technology projects. |
| An image-first route that treats projects as a body of work. | A project view with scope, status, links, and visual context. |
| Studio evidence | Creative technology work |
| Engineering evidence | Production engineering |
| Creative systems section | Creative technology |
| Software as studio practice | Browser-based creative tools and public experiments |
| Where engineering meets creativity | Production software alongside creative technology work |
| Strong fit for teams that value execution and taste | Relevant for teams building cultural products, platforms, tools, and interfaces |

## Audience Balance

For engineering readers, foreground:

- production experience
- senior frontend judgement
- React and TypeScript depth
- API and platform boundaries
- design systems
- delivery inside real organisations
- reliability, maintainability, and clarity

For creative readers, foreground:

- browser instruments
- Web Audio
- sound sketches
- cultural calendars
- archive explorers
- astrology and symbolic interfaces
- public experiments
- artist-facing software

Do not make the creative work sound like a side hobby.

Do not make the engineering work sound like a CV keyword block.

## Artist Variation Writing Spec

For `/variations/artist`:

- Hero eyebrow should mention use, audience, or status.
- Hero tagline should be 3-7 words.
- Supporting copy must include both delivery and artistic signal.
- Panel labels should describe what can be judged.
- Project captions should begin with scope, status, or context.
- Contact copy should name collaboration types or role fit.

Good hero eyebrow:

> Public tools, sound sketches, archive interfaces, and production software.

Good hero tagline:

> Software as cultural material.

Maybe too abstract on its own, but acceptable if the paragraph is concrete.

Better:

> Tools, archives, instruments.

Good supporting sentence:

> I build production software and browser-based creative tools, from frontend platforms and cultural calendars to sound sketches, astrology interfaces, and archive explorers.

Avoid:

> A body of work exploring the poetic and technical dimensions of software.

## Final Check

Before publishing any page, remove any sentence that sounds like it could describe 10,000 other portfolios.

The copy should make it clear that this is specifically Jeremy’s work:

- production React/TypeScript/platform engineering
- cultural and media product context
- sound and Web Audio experiments
- archive and calendar interfaces
- astrology and symbolic systems
- artist-facing tools
- software that is practical, public, and maintainable
